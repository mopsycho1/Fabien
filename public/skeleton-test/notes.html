Round two (30 Oct): the three-quarter sheets

Use the buttons above to switch between Down-right (his front three-quarter sheet), Up-right (his back three-quarter sheet) and round one. Face left mirrors them, so two sheets give all four battle directions. The frames on the right are rows 1 and 2 of his thrust sheet.

Better than round one

  • He now stands the way he does in battle, in three-quarter view, and he faces his target in all four directions.
  • He twists into the thrust. Mid-thrust the chest and head swap to the "turned further" pictures from the sheet.
  • The cape, skirt and mantle are in strips that sway on their own. They swing back when he lunges, overshoot and settle.
  • His knees bend into a real lunge, and his feet stay planted.
  • Small download: about 60 KB per view at game size.

Still short of the frames

  • The twist is a jump between two pictures, not a smooth turn. Three or four angles of the chest and head would smooth it.
  • The sheets weren't quite right:
    • The thighs were drawn as whole legs down to the sandal, so I cut them at the knee.
    • The shins came with feet attached.
    • Everything was drawn about twice the size of the reference figure.
    I put him together by eye against that reference figure.
  • The spear's tassels are painted hanging down, so the spear is turned over when it points the other way.
  • In the frames his whole outline changes, and that still reads as more alive than parts turning.

Where this leaves it

Round two is good enough for the moves where the body doesn't change shape much: breathing, flinching when hit, small weapon sways, walking. Big signature moves (Sonic Javelin, finishers) still look best frame by frame. A mix of the two is possible, but every character would then need two parts sheets as well as the move sheets.

Round one (29 Oct): the front-view sheet

  • The parts joined cleanly and moved smoothly, but it looked like a paper puppet. Flat parts in front view can't twist the body.
  • It was front view only, while the battle uses four diagonal views. That's what the three-quarter sheets fixed.
