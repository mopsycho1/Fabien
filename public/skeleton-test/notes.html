What worked

  • The parts join up cleanly at the pegs. The mantle, skirt and cape hide the seams, so standing still he looks like the drawing.
  • Smooth at any speed (try Slow motion). The computer fills in every in-between, so there are no jumps between pictures.
  • New moves need no new art. The breathing and the thrust are a few lines of numbers each. The feet stay planted by themselves and the front hand finds the shaft by itself.
  • Small download: one view's parts at game size is about 80 KB. One move drawn frame by frame in four directions is about 1.3 MB.

Where it falls short

  • It's a paper puppet. In the frames he turns his shoulders and hips into the thrust. Flat parts can only slide and turn, so the skeleton can't twist the body. This is the biggest gap.
  • Front view only. The battle shows every unit from four diagonal views, so a skeleton needs a parts sheet for a front three-quarter view and a back three-quarter view (the other two are mirror images).
  • The feather cape and skirt swing as single stiff pieces. In the frames every feather moves. Cutting each into 3 to 5 strips would help.
  • Small mismatches in the sheet: the arms and legs are drawn a little smaller than the body (scaled up by eye), the knee joint sits mid-calf where the trousers end, and the arms are drawn already bent.

Recommendation

Frame by frame for the big moves, because it can twist the body. The skeleton is worth it for idle breathing and small reactions (flinches, weapon sway), but only once there's a parts sheet for each three-quarter view.

If you want to push it: one three-quarter parts sheet (Gaha facing down-right), with the cape and skirt cut into strips, and I'll redo this test in that view. Spine (the paid tool) adds bending meshes that would fix the stiff feathers, but only in its Professional version.
